BADSHAHI MMASJID

Badshahi Mosque in Lahore, Pakistan, was built between 1671 and 1673 by the Mughal Emperor Aurangzeb. Located near the Lahore Fort, it is a major landmark, holding up to 55,000 to 100,000 worshippers. It was the world's largest mosque for over 300 years

HISTORY

  1. Built by the sixth Mughal Emperor, Aurangzeb.
  2. Supervised by his foster brother, Muzaffar Hussain (Nawab Fidai Khan Koka).
  3. Used as a military post by the Sikh Empire and British Raj before being restored to Muslims.
  4. Major repairs happened from 1939 to 1960 to fix old damage

ARCHITECHURE

  1. Made from carved red sandstone with white marble inlay
  2. Features three large white marble domes.
  3. Has four tall octagonal minarets at the corners, standing about 53 meters (176 feet) high.
  4. Includes one of the biggest open-air courtyards in the world, spanning over 5.2 acres
  5. The inside prayer hall has rich fresco paintings and marble relief work.

MINAR-A-PAKISTAN

Minar-e-Pakistan is a 70-meter (230 feet) tall national monument tower in Greater Iqbal Park, Lahore, Pakistan. Built between 1960 and 1968, it marks the exact site where the All-India Muslim League passed the historic Lahore Resolution on March 23, 1940, calling for a separate Muslim homeland.

HISTORY

  1. The Resolution: On March 23, 1940, Muslim leaders met at Minto Park (now Greater Iqbal Park). They passed the Lahore Resolution (Qarardad-e-Pakistan).
  2. The Goal: This was the first clear call to divide British India and create a separate state for Muslims.
  3. This meeting set the path for the creation of Pakistan seven years later on August 14, 1947.

ARCHITECHURE

  1. The Base: The base is shaped like a five-pointed flower and consists of four ascending platforms, each telling a story of the freedom struggle using different stones:
  2. The Tower: Made of reinforced concrete with marble and floral panels, the upward-reaching shaft blends traditional Islamic and Mughal aesthetics with modern design.
  3. Interior: Visitors can use stairs or an elevator to reach the top for a wide view of Lahore, and the base houses a museum depicting Pakistan’s history.

RANIKOT FORT

Ranikot Fort, known as the "Great Wall of Sindh", is a massive historical structure in Jamshoro District, Sindh, Pakistan. It is believed to be the largest fort in the world, with a wall circumference of about 32 to 35 kilometers (20 to 22 miles) crossing rugged hills

HISTORY

  1. Built in the early 19th century (around 1812) by the Talpur rulers.
  2. Made of stone and lime mortar
  3. Features solid bastions and four entry gates.
  4. Includes an inner fortress called Miri Fort, which served as a royal residence

ARCITECHURE

  1. Contour hugging: Walls run along three sides of the hilly terrain; high northern peaks act as natural barriers.
  2. Bastions: Solid semi-circular and intermittent watchtowers reinforce the ramparts.
  3. Zig-zag design: Angles improve defensive sightlines and firing range.

MAKLI NECROPOLIS

The Makli Necropolis is one of the world's largest burial sites. It covers 10 square kilometers near Thatta in Sindh, Pakistan. It holds roughly 500,000 to 1 million tombs. These tombs were built over 400 years from the 14th to the 18th century.

HISTORY

  1. Vast Scale: Covers roughly 10 square kilometers on the Makli Hill plateau near the historical city of Thatta.
  2. Timeline: Developed over a 400-year period roughly from the 14th century (Samma dynasty) through the 18th century (Mughal era).
  3. Spiritual Roots: Started around the shrine of the Sufi mystic Hamad al-Jamali, making it a sacred burial place.

ARCHITECHURE

  1. Funerary Canopies (Chattris): Domed yellow sandstone structures standing on pillars, resembling open pavilions.
  2. Pyramidal Tombs: Multi-tiered 16th-century structures featuring minarets and floral motifs unique to the Tarkhan dynasty.
  3. Massive Mausoleums: Imposing square or rectangular brick and stone structures built for royalty and prominent Sufi saints

KHYBER PASS

The Khyber Pass is a famous 33-mile (53 km) mountain pass in the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province of Pakistan. It cuts through the Spin Ghar mountains, connecting the Peshawar Valley at Jamrud to the border post at Torkham on the frontier with Afghanistan

HISTORY

  1. Silk Road Link: Became a vital commercial route for silk, spices, and jade moving between China, Persia, and India.
  2. Early Invaders: Traversed by Persians under Darius I, Greeks under Alexander the Great (326 BCE), and Central Asian nomads like the Kushans.
  3. Cultural Exchange: Facilitated the spread of Buddhism toward Central Asia and Hellenistic influences into South Asia.

ARCHITECHURE

  1. Bab-e-Khyber (The Khyber Gate): A monumental stone arch built in 1964 near Jamrud that serves as a symbolic, fortress-inspired gateway marking the entrance to the pass.
  2. Jamrud Fort: A 19th-century fortification built by the Sikh Empire in 1836 featuring thick stone defensive walls and bastions located near the mouth of the pass.
  3. Shagai Fort: A strategic British-era military fort constructed in 1928 on a high vantage point to control the valley and command surrounding heights.
  4. Ali Masjid Fort and Mosque: A compact defensive post wedged in the narrowest gorge of the pass, designed to restrict movement through the tightest chokepoint.
